❓ FAQ: Time-Based Patterns

Q: What do the hour numbers mean?
Hour numbers correspond to IST (Indian Standard Time):
- Hour 9: 9:00 AM - 10:00 AM IST (Market opens at 9:15 AM)
- Hour 10: 10:00 AM - 11:00 AM IST
- Hour 11: 11:00 AM - 12:00 PM IST
- Hour 12: 12:00 PM - 1:00 PM IST
- Hour 13: 1:00 PM - 2:00 PM IST
- Hour 14: 2:00 PM - 3:00 PM IST
- Hour 15: 3:00 PM - 4:00 PM IST (Market closes at 3:30 PM)

Note: Hours 0-8 are pre-market, and hours 16-23 are post-market. Only hours 9-15 contain trading data.

❓ FAQ: Price Movement Patterns

Q: What are momentum and reversal patterns?
Momentum Patterns:
- 3 Up: Three consecutive candles with positive returns - tests if momentum continues
- 3 Down: Three consecutive candles with negative returns - tests if momentum continues
- Continuation Rate: Percentage of times the pattern continues in the same direction

Reversal Patterns:
- Hammer: Long lower wick (>2x body), small upper wick - bullish reversal signal
- Shooting Star: Long upper wick (>2x body), small lower wick - bearish reversal signal
- Success Rate: Percentage of times the reversal actually occurred (price moved in predicted direction)

❓ FAQ: Algo Trading Pattern Analysis

Q: How are algo trading patterns identified?
Iceberg Pattern Timing: Analyzes when iceberg orders (large hidden orders) occur. Clustering in specific time windows suggests systematic algo trading.

Volume Anomalies: Volume spikes >3 standard deviations above 20-period average - indicates unusual activity, potentially from large algo orders.

Price Anomalies: Price movements >3 standard deviations - sudden, unexpected moves often from algo execution.

Combined Anomalies: Simultaneous high volume (>2.5σ) AND high price movement (>2.5σ) - strongest indicator of algo trading activity.

Peak Activity Hours: Hours with highest concentration of anomalies indicate when quant traders/algo systems are most active.

This refers to the last 7 unique trading days (excluding weekends and holidays), not calendar days. For example, if today is Friday, it includes the last 7 weekdays with trading activity.

- Daily OHLC (Open, High, Low, Close) prices
- Daily returns and price ranges

Q: How accurate is the prediction model?
The prediction model uses an ensemble of 4 different approaches to forecast next 3 trading days:

1. Linear Trend Model: Extrapolates recent return trend using linear regression
2. Moving Average Model: Uses average of recent returns
3. Weighted Average Model: Gives more weight to recent days (30% most recent)
4. Price Trend Model: Analyzes price movement and converts to expected returns

Important: This is a simple trend-following model based on historical patterns. Real markets are complex and unpredictable. Use predictions as one input among many, not as definitive forecasts. Past performance does not guarantee future results.

Q: What are Smart Money features?
Synthetic IV (Implied Volatility): Estimated volatility using Parkinson's method from High/Low price ranges. Higher values indicate increased uncertainty.

Smart Money Flow (Detrended OBV): On-Balance Volume minus its 200-period moving average. Positive values suggest accumulation, negative suggests distribution.

Sentiment Skew: Skewness of returns over a rolling window. Positive skew = greed/optimism, negative skew = fear/pessimism.
Q: What are the different pattern types?
Hidden Accumulation: Large volume with small price movement - institutions buying without moving price.

Hidden Distribution: Large volume with small price movement - institutions selling without moving price.

Bullish Sweep: Price breaks above recent high with volume - potential bullish momentum.

Bearish Sweep: Price breaks below recent low with volume - potential bearish momentum.

Iceberg: Very high volume (>3σ) with minimal price change - large hidden orders being executed slowly.

Capitulation: Extreme selling pressure with high volume and negative sentiment - potential reversal point.
Q: How is the prediction model calculated?
The prediction model uses an ensemble approach combining 4 different models:

1. Linear Trend Model: Fits a linear regression to recent returns and extrapolates forward.

2. Moving Average Model: Uses the simple average of recent returns as prediction.

3. Weighted Average Model: Gives more weight to recent days (30% most recent, decreasing weights for older days).

4. Price Trend Model: Analyzes price movement trend and converts to expected returns.

The final prediction is the average of all 4 models. Note: This is a simple trend-following model and should be used with caution. Real markets are complex and unpredictable.
Q: What are anomalies and how are they detected?
Volume Anomalies: Volume spikes that are >3 standard deviations above the 20-period rolling average. These indicate unusual trading activity, potentially from large institutional orders or algo trading.

Price Anomalies: Price movements that are >3 standard deviations above the 20-period rolling average of absolute returns. These indicate sudden, unexpected price moves.

Combined Anomalies: Simultaneous high volume (>2.5σ) AND high price movement (>2.5σ). These are strong indicators of algorithmic trading activity or large institutional moves.

Q: How should I interpret the time heatmap?
The time heatmap shows average returns by hour and day of week.

- Green cells: Positive average returns for that hour-day combination
- Red cells: Negative average returns
- Darker colors: Stronger returns (positive or negative)
- Brighter colors: Weaker returns

Use this to identify:
- Best hours to trade (consistently green)
- Worst hours to trade (consistently red)
- Day-specific patterns (e.g., Friday afternoons might show different patterns)
Q: What is the difference between 'Simple Analysis' and 'Smart Money Analysis'?
Simple Analysis: Uses basic Z-score calculations on raw OHLCV data to detect patterns like Panic Sells, FOMO Buys, etc.

Smart Money Analysis: Uses advanced features (Synthetic IV, Detrended OBV, Sentiment Skew) to detect more sophisticated institutional patterns like Hidden Accumulation/Distribution, Liquidity Sweeps, and Iceberg orders. This is more accurate for identifying professional trading activity.
